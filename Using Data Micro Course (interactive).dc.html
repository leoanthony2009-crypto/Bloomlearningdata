Common data problem

Schools often collect survey, assessment and attendance data — but the data does not always direct decisions, follow-up or accountability.

Scorecard response

A balanced scorecard keeps a small number of improvement priorities visible, linked to evidence, owners, actions, review dates and movement over time.

DataWhat does the evidence say?
DecisionWhat matters most now?
ActionWho will do what by when?
ReviewWhat changed and what did we learn?

Example from Management & Monitoring

Objective: strengthen leadership monitoring and documentation so assessment evidence is consistently reviewed, acted upon and revisited for impact.

Key leadership shift

From: “We had a meeting.”

To: “The meeting produced decisions, actions, follow-up dates and evidence of impact.”

Try it: rewrite one line from your own action plan

Take a “we had a meeting” line and rewrite it so it names the decision, the owner, the follow-up date and the evidence you would expect.
ActivityWhat the school does.
Implementation evidenceProof the activity happened.
Outcome evidenceProof it made a difference.
IndicatorThe measure used to judge progress.
Worked example — Management & Monitoring. Activity: monthly assessment-review meetings. Implementation: minutes and action tracker. Outcome: decisions followed up and revisited. Indicator: % of agreed actions completed by the next review.

A hypothesis turns a data finding into a testable leadership claim. Next term, leaders ask: Did the action happen? Did it help? What should change?
